# 2026-10-04: Luganda speech-recognition smoke test

> **Small hackathon evaluation, not clinical validation.** Three real clips cannot estimate accuracy. This shows that a small Luganda model runs offline, what it gets right and wrong on real speech, and that TuWulira's safety layer turns unusable audio into "Not sure. Please ask a person." instead of keeping invented words.

Machine-readable result: [2026-10-04-luganda-asr-smoke.json](2026-10-04-luganda-asr-smoke.json). How to re-run: [evaluation/luganda-asr/](../luganda-asr/README.md).

## Results on real Luganda speech

Official transcripts are copied exactly from Common Voice. The team did not write or translate any Luganda.

| Clip | Speaker | Official transcript | Model heard | Verdict | Confidence | Time (clip length) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `common_voice_lg_23704551.mp3` | man, 20s | Ekirwadde kino kitadde nnyo abantu ku **bunkenke**. | Ekirwadde kino kitadde nnyo abantu ku **bwenkenge**. | Close: 1 of 7 words wrong | 0.802 | 4.7 s (5.3 s)* |
| `common_voice_lg_23704504.mp3` | man, 20s, another speaker | Ekikajjo **kimu** ku birime ebirimu ssente ennaku zino. | Ekikajjo **kino** ku birime ebirimu ssente ennaku zino. | Close: 1 of 8 words wrong | 0.910 | 4.3 s (4.5 s) |
| `common_voice_lg_23710054.mp3` | woman, 20s | Wano we muleese omulwadde wammwe tewaliiwo ddagala. | Wano we muleese omulwadde wammwe tewaliiwo ddagala. | Correct | 0.839 | 4.9 s (5.4 s) |

Overall: **2 of 22 words wrong (word error rate 0.091)**, 1 of 3 clips exactly right. All three passed the safety layer as usable speech.

\* The first clip includes one-off warm-up time.

## Failure clips: silence and noise

| Clip | What it is | Model heard | Confidence | TuWulira safety layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `synthetic_silence_01.wav` | 5 s of near-silence (quiet-room noise at -60 dBFS, generated by the script; in the repo) | "Abaggwa baggwa mu ggwanga ggwanga." | 0.252 | **"Not sure. Please ask a person."** |
| `common_voice_lg_23704551.noisy.real.wav` | The first real clip with loud white noise added (speech 5 dB quieter than the noise). Still holds a real voice, so local only |  "Baddukira ssanyu nnyo nnyo." | 0.387 | **"Not sure. Please ask a person."** |

**The model invented Luganda words from silence and from noise** (2 of 2). This is a known behaviour of Whisper-type models. Both were caught: low confidence sent them to "ask a person", so nothing would be saved to the card. **This is why the safety layer must stay separate from the model.**

## What this does and does not show

Shows:

- A small (151 MB) Luganda model transcribes real Luganda read speech, mostly correctly, with no internet after download.
- On silence and noise it invents words, and TuWulira's confidence check catches both.

Does not show, and limitations:

- **Not clinical validation.** Three read-aloud sentences, not patients describing symptoms, not a clinic waiting room, not children or older voices.
- **Too few clips for an accuracy figure.** The 0.091 word error rate is for 22 words only. The repo's plan is 20 to 30 clips and a comparison with a published benchmark (metrics.md, RQ3.1).
- **Possible overlap with training data.** The model card says it was trained on Common Voice 16.1, but not which splits. We used the 17.0 test split, which is meant to be kept out of training, but cannot confirm it was.
- **Near the threshold.** The clip with a wrong word (`bwenkenge`) scored 0.802, just above the 0.8 threshold, so a wrong word would have passed. The threshold is one global number and still needs calibrating per field (RQ4.2). On the card, the main problem is always shown as "patient reported", with the original words one tap away.
- **Laptop, not phone.** Speed and memory were measured on a laptop with PyTorch in float32. The repo's target is an int8 model under about 120 MB on an itel A50 (models/README.md); a phone run with a quantized model and an on-device runtime (for example sherpa-onnx) is still to do (RQ6.1).
- **Confidence measure.** "Confidence" here is the model's own average probability for the word-pieces it produced. It is one simple choice; others (for example a voice-activity check before transcription) were not tested.
- **One model only.** Larger Luganda models (for example Sunbird's, about 1.26 GB) were not tried because of size.
